Goldshire: reference against build

Every pair is a Blizzard screenshot beside the frame this build renders of the same thing. The screenshots on the right come from node tools/shoot.mjs --out refs/after and are deterministic: the clock, the player and the animation phase all freeze under a ?cam= preset, so any difference between two runs is a change to the world.

Where the build knowingly departs from the reference, it says so under the pair. A board that only shows agreement proves nothing.

The inn

What this settles: roof sag and overhang, corner post flare, where the lozenges sit, the chimney. The eave now clears the wall plate by geometry rather than by luck: eaveY came down to 5.70 when overhangZ went to 1.5, and ?wire=1 in after/verify/ shows the overhang is real geometry with no open slot at the junction. The lozenge run moved off the facade bays and onto the flared posts, which is where every reference has it.

The authored inn is 16 x 10 m. The minimap's 59 x 33 m blob is a merged bounding box over the inn, the stable and a neighbour, so it is not a wall measurement to match. The horses at the right are Stage 8; the reference has an animal at the inn in every wide shot.

What this settles: the belt beam, plaster tone and post flare read from the porch side. Light timber moved from #8a6a44 to #b1834e and the plaster warmed with it.

What this settles: window glow and lantern falloff. The bloom pass needs the emissive drive above the diffuse ceiling, not a clever threshold: sunlit plaster at sunIntensity 1.5 already exceeds 1.0, so any threshold that spares plaster also spares every window. Night emissives run 2.8 to 6.2 against a threshold of 1.55.

What this settles: bowed ceiling beams, the brick hearth and the plank tone. Interior brick went from #a3462f to the reference's much hotter #b5571e, and the straight beam() calls became bowed ones.

The smithy

What this settles: the smithy is pale ashlar to the eave, not stone to waist height with plaster above, and its loudest feature is the riveted plate hood over the coals. What was here before was a 22 cm stove pipe. The mouth is 1.15 m, which is the widest bell that still clears the chimney tower at 2.8 m centres.

What this settles: crenellated top, light border, gold gryphon, pointed tail, hung from a timber arm on a free-standing pole. Built as a vertex grid rather than a plane with an alpha-cut map: the silhouette is the whole point of a pennant, and an alpha-tested material would be a second material instance that the static merge cannot bucket with anything else.

The banner faces south-east, square to the junction approach. That is the only view it exists to serve; from the smithy's own close-up preset the cloth is close to edge-on.

The junction

What this settles: the arrangement. Smithy left, inn away to the right, cobble apron under the junction with dirt verges running out of it, split rail along the road. The smithy moved from the south-west to the north-west, the inn from (20, -18) to (49, -21), and the invented town hall was deleted: Goldshire has never had one.

What this settles: road topology. Bearings were measured off map/gs_zoom_marked.png by walking circles of road pixels around the junction rather than read off prose: 271° west, 120° east, 5° north to the fork, then 321° for Stormwind and 23° for Northshire. The layout lint reports the built bearings so a coordinate edit cannot quietly break one.

Crystal Lake's real west shore is around x = 153, past the fog wall at this density. Its west shore sits at x = 125 here, which reads from the shore road, and the terrain stays 320 m at a 2 m grid rather than growing to 480 and under-sampling every road edge.

What this settles: the peaked hexagonal board on two heavy posts, with a timber X across a pinned face and a small shingled cap over the apex. The cap's pitch and half-length are chosen so its outer ends land on the frame's own slope instead of overhanging the face.

What this settles: red canvas slung between poles, sagging, standing free on the verge rather than fixed to the cart. Tharynn's crates and sacks moved under it.

The outbuildings and the forest

What this settles: open-shed construction and post proportions. Closed on the south and both gables, wide open to the road so the stalls, the hay and the tack all read from it.

What this settles: trunk scale and the canopy over the road. Trees were at roughly half scale; Elwynn trunks are 2 to 4 m thick with the first branch 15 to 20 m up. Crown card size is now a fraction of tree height rather than an absolute metre value, so raising the height cannot leave a small crown on a tall trunk.

What this settles: the palette numbers. Every colour in the project is a CSS hex string in one file, sampled here against verified crops. The shingle grid also coarsened from 8 x 12 to 6 x 8: on the inn roof that is 0.25 m of course exposure against a reference reading of 0.30, where the old grid gave 0.19 and read as fish scale from the road.

What was checked, and how

Layout invariants node tools/lint-layout.mjs No pad overlaps, no fence or torch on paving, road bearings as measured, 77 props all at least 1.6 m clear of every road and pad
Collider holes node tools/lint-collider.mjs Inn ground, upper and cellar decks, the cellar flight, the roost switchback and 326 road samples: no hole, road grade worst 3 cm off the terrain's own answer
Frame cost node tools/perf.mjs Vsync-bound at 120 Hz in every preset, before the work and after it. ?cam=aerial went from 331 draws and 287k triangles to 352 and 300k across all nine stages
Bloom at noon after/time/t13.png Nothing blooms but the sun. The cressets now go out at sunrise, which they did not before: their lights were already scaled by darkness but the flame cards were not

Determinism after/hashes.json No Math.random() anywhere in the project; every scatter takes a seeded generator, so two runs of the shoot harness agree byte for byte

Open: the layout lint reports 6 of 400 pond samples above the waterline, where the shore wobble reaches inside the sample grid. It is cosmetic at the pond's scale and unchanged since the shoreline work.
